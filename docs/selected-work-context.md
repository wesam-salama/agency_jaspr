## 2. How to use this context

Work in order within each chapter. The ten steps define the business brief, strategy, visual language, content, application, details, assets, proof and handoff. Complete one step’s decisions before generating dependent assets. For example, approve the garment canon before producing Fold photography, and approve Sift’s flat label artwork before applying it to a bag mockup.

This file contains **creative specifications**, **fictional business/content fixtures**, and **future evidence requirements**. Keep them distinguishable:

- Creative specifications lock the name, positioning, palette, type roles, motif and composition direction. Treat them as authoritative for generation unless the user explicitly changes them.
- Fixtures make the concept coherent: product records, study names, course curriculum, sample prices and stock. They are not supplier statements, transactions or real client records.
- Evidence requirements identify what must actually be created, implemented, captured or checked before using completion language in the public case.

All four names are working brand names adopted for the studio concepts. Domain, business-name and trademark clearance are unverified. Do not invent available domains, physical addresses, founder biographies, certification badges, real customers or corporate suffixes.

Use the internal status sequence **planned → designed → prototyped → implemented demo → verified**. A print mockup may be designed while the app is merely planned; status is recorded per deliverable, not automatically promoted for an entire project. Real client commissioning, commercial launch and actual business results are separate facts that require their own evidence.

The public label is **Studio concept** on a card and **Self-initiated studio project** in case details. A verified demo remains self-initiated; producing it does not create an external client history. The content should sound like careful authored work because its details are specific, not because it fabricates delivery evidence.

2. **Identity:** create editable marks, colour/type rules, small-size examples and English/Arabic hierarchy.

6. **Case assembly:** generate narrative, captions and alt text from the completion record; verify the asset/copy match; localize the approved meaning before website integration.

Use text contrast of at least 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for qualifying large text; important control/state graphics require 3:1 against the relevant adjacent colour. ### Step 01 — Define the business and the design problem

Sift Coffee is a proposed specialty coffee brand for home brewers who want clear choices. Its first collection contains three coffees with different flavour directions and recommended starting methods. Subscriptions, equipment and educational content are possible later extensions, outside this first case.

**Confirmed direction:** international, with English master content and Arabic-ready packaging. Bilingual print applications are specified below. Primary customers are curious home brewers buying their first grinder or improving their daily cup. Secondary customers are experienced brewers who appreciate a well-edited selection. Demand remains unresearched.

The core problem is information hierarchy. Sift Coffee should answer four questions quickly: which coffee is this, what might it taste like, how should I start brewing it, and which format am I buying?

**Design goals:** establish a recognisable identity without bean illustrations; distinguish three coffees without producing three unrelated brands; make factual product information easy to find; and maintain equal care across English and Arabic surfaces. Proposed services are consultancy, positioning, naming rationale, verbal identity, visual identity, packaging and brand guidelines. A website or app is not required to prove this project's scope.

### Step 02 — Establish the strategy and the name

**Positioning statement:** For curious home brewers, Sift is a carefully edited coffee brand that makes choosing and brewing feel clear, through a small collection, useful guidance and precise design.

Everyday provides a dependable starting point, Daybreak introduces a brighter flavour direction, and After Hours provides a deeper one. The collection invites experimentation while remaining understandable to someone seeking a good daily coffee.

**Name rationale:** “Sift” suggests selecting, refining and finding what matters. Sift Coffee connects to the studio's emphasis on detail without illustrating a sieve. Business-name, domain and trademark availability remain unchecked.

**Brand promise:** “Good coffee. Clearly chosen.” Use this as the primary tagline, with its punctuation preserved. Supporting line: “Three coffees. Three clear starting points.” Avoid positioning Sift as the best, healthiest, most sustainable or ethically superior without supporting evidence.

**Voice:** warm, direct and quietly curious. Explain technical terms when they affect a choice. Prefer “A bright cup with apricot and citrus notes” to unexplained processing jargon. Address the brewer as “you”; avoid invented founder stories, testimonials and sourcing relationships.

**Arabic presentation:** retain “Sift Coffee” and use the descriptive line “قهوة مختارة بعناية”. Review final Arabic copy before production artwork.

### Step 03 — Set the creative concept

**Creative concept: The Selected Line.** A small group of parallel horizontal rules contains one slightly longer, cobalt rule. It represents the act of identifying a useful choice within a collection. The arrangement is deliberately simple enough to become a label divider, an image caption device, a bag side-panel motif or a tiny digital icon.

Build the motif from a five-rule unit on an eight-part width grid. Four rules occupy six parts; the selected rule occupies eight. Keep the longer rule in the middle for the master motif, and move it only when a layout uses it to point to an actual selected item. The motif is a graphic device, not a taste score or measurement.

The visual mood is a contemporary pantry: warm paper, dark type, restrained cobalt and roasted orange. Product photographs communicate material and scale through folded paper, a spoon, a cup and a bag on a real surface.

Large typography establishes the product; quieter rules and explanatory copy reveal the care close up. Paper and photography add material variation, while typography and spacing provide consistency.
